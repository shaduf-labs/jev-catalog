Failure self-check, checklist question 9, and Drex on alternatives (20 Oct 2026)

This dated report records the evidence behind the update of 20 October 2026. It adds a self-check to When Jev fails that sets your own failure handling next to the 80 implementations on the map, adds a ninth checklist question that the map cannot count, corrects sentences that still gave the earlier count of 79, and reads Drex (Nace.AI) for the alternatives page. Nothing was run: no account, key, API call or third-party code.

Key findings

Self-check: for each of five failure conditions you pick what your code does and see how many of the 80 made the same choice, with up to three example rows. It compares choices only; it does not read or test code, and nothing you pick leaves your browser.

Documentedcounts taken from the map as recomputed 19 Oct 2026; every count in the self-check equals the same count on the map page (checked by script, 20 Oct 2026)

One choice no implementation made: with no API key, 0 of 80 return no decision (51 refuse, error or hold; 17 fall back; 9 proceed as if allowed; 3 are advisory).

Documentedsource at pinned commits, map of 19 Oct 2026 (51 + 17 + 9 + 0 + 3 = 80)

Question 9: "Does your code log or show when it falls back or fails open?" Not counted across the 80: the map does not record whether a fallback is visible. Timeouts (question 2) and logging (question 8) are not tallied either.

Documenteda limit of the map's columns, 20 Oct 2026

Alternatives: Of 38 listed substitutes: 13 model weights, 11 local runtimes, 4 request adapters, 2 classifier/LLM approaches, 8 hosted APIs from other providers (OpenAI, Microsoft, Cloudflare, Subquadratic, Liquid AI, Mouse, Upright, Nace.AI).

Documentedcounts checked by script over the table rows, 20 Oct 2026 (13 + 11 + 4 + 2 + 8 = 38)

Access: unchanged at 20 Oct 2026, 05:13 UTC. jev-latest still points to jev-1.13.0 and jev-preview to jev-1.14.0.

DocumentedTypeSafe models page and status page, 20 Oct 2026, 05:13 UTC (R20-S04, R20-S05)

What the self-check compares, and what it does not

  • It compares five choices: what your code does on a Jev error or timeout, on a malformed answer, below its threshold, with no API key, and which code paths skip Jev. Each choice is shown in the map's own terms (fail-closed, fallback, fail-open, no-decision, advisory; held, acts-anyway, no-threshold below a threshold).
  • What comes back: "N of 80 made the same choice", the map term, up to three example rows (name, domain and pinned commit, as on the map), and the checklist question that goes with it. A fallback choice also points to question 7 (is the fallback cheaper or riskier than failing?). "Not sure / not checked" points to the checklist question that shows what to look for.
  • What it does not do: it does not read, run or test your code, does not call Jev, stores nothing and sends nothing. It does not say whether a choice suits your use; the counts describe the 80 implementations at their pinned commits, not a rate for Jev integrations in general.
  • Without JavaScript: the same counts are in the checklist directly below the self-check, and every row is in the All 80 rows table.

What the map does not record

  • Whether a fallback or fail-open is visible (logged or shown to a person): the map has no column for it. It is now checklist question 9, with no count.
  • Timeouts (question 2): examples are quoted, but timeouts were not tallied across the 80.

  • Real-world behaviour: every cell is a reading of source at a pinned commit; none of the 80 was run by this site.

Drex (Nace.AI)

Drex is a hosted decision model from Nace.AI with open weights for two of its versions; it is listed on alternatives under its primary layer, a hosted API from another provider, with the open weights as a second layer. It is not published by TypeSafe and is not Jev-named.

Status and shape
"Generally available" (launch post, 24 Sep 2026). POST https://console.nace.ai/v1/systemone with a bearer key; 1–512 questions; state up to 131,072 tokens. Documented Nace's docs say Drex is "wire-compatible with TypeSafe's Jev" and works with the TypeSafe SDK "without a fork"; this is the vendor's statement and was not tested. Reported
Price as published
Console pricing docs: drex-v2.0 $0.06, drex-v1.5 $0.05 and drex-max $0.06 per 1M input tokens; output "not billed" (read 20 Oct 2026, 05:17 UTC). The product page gives drex-v1.5 as "Cost per 1,000 decisions $0.04", a different unit; the two are not reconciled here. New accounts get "$25.00 of signup credit" per Nace, an access condition, not a statement of cost. Documented
Your data
Service Terms (last updated 19 Oct 2026): "No training by default"; deletion "within 48 hours after processing completes for Paid Accounts and within 7 days after processing completes for Free Tier Accounts". A reading of the text, not legal advice. Documented
Weights
nace-ai/drex-v2.0 (3e6d164) and nace-ai/drex-v1.5 (f80f9d2) under the "NACE AI OPEN RAIL-M LICENSE (MODIFIED)", which requires a separate commercial licence above $1,000,000 in revenue or funding, except for personal use or research. Base models per the cards (Reported; their licences not read). Documented
Claims against Jev
Nace.AI sells a competing hosted decision model. Its own figures: Drex 2.0 "61.25 vs 57.96" against Jev 1.13.0 on Decision Index 0.3.1, and Drex Max "6.9 points over Jev 1.14". No graded comparison exists. Reported

Not read: Nace.AI's website Terms and Privacy pages and its Console data processing addendum (opened, not read), the terms of the provider behind the OpenRouter route, the Drex Max launch-post body, the base-model licences. No Drex model was called.

Version checks and other edits

  • LiteLLM 1.106.0 (published 18 Oct, tag commit 955bdaa): the Jev classifier's failure path is unchanged from 1.104.0; the classifier type is renamed oss_classifier (old names accepted) and failures now record a reason in the routing log. Files compared as text; nothing installed. No map cell changed. Documented (frameworks)
  • n8n 2.44.3: activating or publishing a workflow is refused when a node that can run has no required credential; a workflow that is already active is re-registered at start-up without this check and stops at execution with "Node does not have any credentials set". Source read at the release tag; nothing run. Documented (n8n)
  • OpenAI's Decisions API guide now accepts image URLs as well as base64 data URLs and is still a public beta (read 20 Oct 2026, 05:24 UTC). No comparison-card cell changed. Documented (Jev vs decision APIs)
  • Other releases in the window were patch releases (ai 7.0.146, @ai-sdk/typesafe-ai 3.0.23, @tanstack/ai-typesafe 0.1.13, n8n 2.44.4); noted, not re-read.
