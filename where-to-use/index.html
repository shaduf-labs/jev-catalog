Where to use Jev (TypeSafe AI) and what it costs: routes compared

You can call Jev directly from TypeSafe or through five gateways. The listed input price is the same or nearly the same on every route that shows one; the routes differ in request shape, context limit, data terms, billing and who you have an account with.

Access today: Signups reopened 27 Sep 2026 (22:33 UTC); new users currently get no free credit. Checked 28 Sep 2026, 19:50 UTC. Documented Full access timeline
Short answer

TypeSafe lists jev-1.13.0 at $0.042 per million input tokens, output free. OpenRouter, Cloudflare and Vercel (in its models API) list the same price; Vercel's web page and Opper's card round it to $0.04; Netlify bills in its own credits at an unknown rate (all checked 28 Sep 2026). Use TypeSafe direct or Opper if you need the 64k request budget; use OpenRouter if you do not want a TypeSafe account; use Cloudflare if you need a route that lists zero data retention. None of these listings proves an account can call Jev or what it will be billed.

Before you pay anyone. Unofficial resellers charge 3–16× the list price; check any other site on official vs reseller sites. To estimate a monthly bill from your own token counts, use the cost per decision worksheet.

Choosing on context and data terms

  • 64k request budget: listed by TypeSafe direct and Opper, and in the text of Vercel's API description (whose context_window field says 32000). OpenRouter, Cloudflare and Netlify list 32k.
  • Zero data retention: Cloudflare's listing says "Yes"; Vercel's API says "none"; Opper says "not established"; TypeSafe offers it to enterprise customers only; OpenRouter's terms for Jev were not established.
  • No TypeSafe account: every gateway; OpenRouter states it explicitly.
  • Exact cost per response: OpenRouter returns usage.cost with each response.

Before you send real data

  1. Pin jev-1.13.0 instead of an alias if your thresholds depend on one model version. Recheck aliases and limits before a pilot.
  2. Use synthetic or redacted inputs until you have checked the account's access, retention, credits and terms. A public listing is not a successful call.
  3. Read the TypeSafe MCA, privacy policy and DPA for your workload. On 27 Sep the MCA showed "last updated 23 Sep" (what changed from 19 Sep is unknown), the privacy policy 19 Nov 2025 and the DPA 24 Apr 2026.
  4. Handle the documented 401, 422, 429 and 529 responses with a fallback in your own code; see errors and rate limits.
  5. Do not copy a context limit from one route to another: TypeSafe's 64k budget is not established for gateways that list 32k.

What was not verified

  • No account on any route was opened, charged or used to call Jev.
  • Netlify's credit conversion, Opper's entitlement, how Jev Router bills routed models, and all actual bills are unknown.
  • Data-retention statements are quoted from listings; the legal documents were not reviewed clause by clause.
  • OpenRouter's retention and logging for Jev, and the text of TypeSafe's MCA, privacy policy and DPA, were not reviewed in this release.
  • All fields were rechecked on 28 September 2026. Listings can change without notice.
