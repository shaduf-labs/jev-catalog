Where can I use Jev, and at what price?

Access routes are not interchangeable. The official model, a gateway, and an independent proxy can have different endpoints, billing, accounts, limits, and data boundaries.

Checked 20 September 2026: the 20 Sep official model record named Jev 1.13, model ID jev-1.13.0, at $0.042 per million input tokens with free output. A waitlist link was observed on 20 Sep; on 24 Sep the fetched home text did not show it. Console access, credits, and eligibility are still unverified. The missing text is not evidence that the waitlist ended.

Official context and data terms

Legal revision trigger · 24 Sep 2026: TypeSafe's MCA displays a 23 Sep 2026 update date. Its current text permits service/fee processing and telemetry and says Customer Data is not used to train model weights without prior consent. We could not determine which clauses changed from the 19 Sep version. Review the current MCA, DPA, and selected route before sending sensitive data.
“Not used to train” is not “not stored.” The checked TypeSafe legal pages say Input is not used to train or fine-tune models without consent. They also allow processing for service delivery, fee calculation, telemetry, fraud/abuse prevention, and legal purposes. The privacy policy says services are hosted in the United States and standard retention lasts as long as reasonably necessary; it does not give a Jev-specific retention duration. Enterprise zero-data-retention is a separate option.
  • Master Customer Agreement: credits and usage limits govern access; service and telemetry processing are described; output can be inaccurate and service is not promised to be uninterrupted.
  • Privacy policy: no training/fine-tuning on prompts or Input, service-provider disclosures, US hosting, and a general retention statement.
  • Data processing addendum: processor terms, purpose-based retention, subprocessors, and international-transfer terms for the relevant customer arrangement.

Before you build against a route

  1. Check eligibility. Confirm that the account can obtain credits and an API key; a login page or waitlist link is not a quota promise.
  2. Pin the model when thresholds matter. Prefer jev-1.13.0 for a reproducible test; use an alias only when automatic upgrades are acceptable.
  3. Measure the real state. Send text-only input, test the 32k state-plus-longest-question constraint, and treat the published rate limits as provisional.
  4. Recheck the data boundary. Gateways and the independent proxy add their own account, logging, retention, billing, and terms. Minimise state and keep a fallback for 429/529 or provider outages.
Cost intuition: input-token pricing is not a price per decision in the abstract. State and question text determine token use; gateway fees, quotas, and proxy packs can change the bill. The independent wrapper's self-reported rates vary by pack ($0.42/M, $0.35/M, or $0.25/M on 24 Sep) and are not official TypeSafe pricing.
