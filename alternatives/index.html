Jev alternatives (TypeSafe AI): open-source models, local runtimes and hosted APIs, and what each replaces

What can replace Jev (TypeSafe AI): open-weight models, local runtimes, request adapters and hosted decision APIs from 8 other providers (OpenAI, Microsoft, Cloudflare, Subquadratic, Liquid AI, Mouse, Upright, Nace.AI), by layer and licence. "Alternative" can mean five different things: a different request library, a local runtime, a different trained model, another provider's hosted API, or a different kind of model altogether. Pick the layer you need to replace first; a matching request shape does not mean matching answers. Why this title: Google completes "jev alt" to "jev alternatives"; Bing and DuckDuckGo complete it to "jev alternative" (autocomplete, 10 Oct 2026, 05:17 UTC; R12-S11). Short answer

Official weights or a local runtime. TypeSafe-published Jev weights or a local Jev runtime: not found on typesafe.ai (no /models page, HTTP 404), the docs (llms.txt, the models page, the introduction), TypeSafe's GitHub organisation (12 repositories: SDKs, an interface adapter, skills, examples, n8n nodes and two forks) or a Hugging Face author search for TypeSafe accounts (0 models), checked 10 Oct 2026, 05:11–05:20 UTC. Documented TypeSafe's docs describe Jev as hosted: "the same weights serve every account". Its new Enterprise page lists the question "Can we run Jev in our own VPC or on-premises?", but the answer is not in the served page and the pool has not read it. (R12-S100–S110, R12-S21, R12-S36, R12-S58)

Can you run Jev in Ollama? Not TypeSafe's Jev on the evidence checked. Ollama's library search for "jev" showed no Jev-named model on its first page (10 Oct 2026, 05:11 UTC), and no TypeSafe weights were found (above). Ollama 0.35 (blog, 29 Sep) runs other Jev-style decision models (nimble from Bespoke Labs; tev1 and tev1:0.8b from Together AI) behind a System One-style endpoint. OllamaMQ v0.5.0 is a queue in front of such servers, not a model. Documented (R12-S107, R12-S135, R12-S136)

Other options. If you need local weights, the independently trained options with inspectable repositories and model cards are Kev, CLM, Laya and Jeff, among others; Kev and CLM also expose a System One-style API. Ollaya (new on 28 Sep) runs several of these models locally behind a System One-compatible API. The pool has run no parity test of its own (nothing run as of 10 Oct 2026); their accuracy figures are the authors' own. Published comparisons with Jev: Opper's Jev–Kev comparison (B11, grade B, operator with a stake). Three comparisons of Jev with OpenAI's Decisions API are graded and differ by task: see its row (B38, komzweb, and B39, mateusfonsek, since 11 Oct 2026; B41, kartikanand73, since 12 Oct 2026; all three grade A); on 24 Oct 2026 there are six (B42, grade B, since 13 Oct; B44, grade A, since 14 Oct; B45, grade A, since 24 Oct). One graded comparison includes Microsoft-Decision-1 (two since 14 Oct 2026, with B44): On synthetic bank-withdrawal routing (B41, grade A), Jev (89.2% macro-F1) and Microsoft-Decision-1 (87.6%) were not separated and both were ahead of OpenAI's Decisions API (71.4%); Decision-1 kept the same route across all 6 option orders more often (97.2–97.6% vs Jev 91.6–91.8%, Decisions API 64.2%). Reported; see its row. Hosted Jev, Laya and Kev are compared on the same rows on Jev vs Laya vs Kev. (This paragraph: rows checked 26 Sep–1 Oct, not rechecked on 10 Oct.)

Another provider's hosted API. Another provider's hosted decision API is a different layer. The table lists 8. OpenAI's Decisions API (generally available since 22 Oct 2026) runs its own model, gpt-6-luna, behind a different request shape, and your data goes to OpenAI (row; R12-S134, R11-S300; status R22-S32, R22-S47). Microsoft-Decision-1 (published 9 Oct 2026) is listed in Microsoft Foundry's catalog and on OpenRouter; it is text-only, and your data goes to Microsoft under Microsoft's terms (row; R14-S208, R14-S209, R14-S212, checked 12 Oct 2026, 05:13 UTC). Cloudflare's Clef family (Clef, Clef-flash, Clef-omni) runs on Workers AI with the same state-plus-questions shape as Jev but a different endpoint, and your data goes to Cloudflare under Cloudflare's terms on Workers AI; on OpenRouter's non-Cloudflare hosts (PrimeIntellect, DeepInfra) it goes to that host (row; R14-S400–S405, checked 12 Oct 2026, 05:33 UTC; R15-S226, 13 Oct 2026, 05:21 UTC); its weights are also published (row). SubQ Snap (Subquadratic, public preview) uses the same request shape as Jev's /v1/systemone on its own endpoint, with its own model; your data goes to Subquadratic (row; R16-S217 to R16-S227, read 14 Oct 2026, 16:47–16:48 UTC). Liquid AI d1-ultra (available) uses Jev's request shape on its own endpoint, Mouse-1 (preview) and Eevo (Upright) each have their own decision API plus a /systemone endpoint in Jev's shape, and your data goes to each vendor under its own terms, which differ on training and retention (d1-ultra, Mouse-1, Eevo rows; R19-S100 to R19-S152, read 19 Oct 2026, 05:22–05:25 UTC). Drex (Nace.AI; "Generally available" per its launch post) uses Jev's request shape on its own endpoint; Nace's docs call it "wire-compatible" with Jev (a vendor statement, not tested). Its weights are also published, under a modified Open RAIL-M licence with revenue and funding thresholds for commercial use, and your data goes to Nace.AI under its own terms (row; R20-S100 to R20-S128, read 20 Oct 2026, 05:16–05:23 UTC). All eight are alternatives, not products built on Jev. See the hosted-API layer. Compare side by side: Decisions API vs Jev vs Microsoft Decision 1.

Choosing between Jev, a general LLM with JSON output and a trained classifier is a different question: see Jev vs an LLM vs a classifier: when to use which. Published accuracy tests, graded by how far they can be checked, are on the benchmarks ledger.
